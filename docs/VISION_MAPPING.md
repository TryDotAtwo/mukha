# Visual input mapping evidence

The primary annotation file contains 6,098 ol_sensory objects. None has assigned
hex coordinates. There are 23,720 coordinate-bearing neurons elsewhere in the
source, mainly columnar interneurons; their locations cannot simply be assigned
to photoreceptors by array order.

The author's release-v1.0 optic-column workbook is acquired from
flyconnectome/2025malecns commit 67767d2233657983993ff6c2be48e836a935863c.
`reports/optic_column_sources.json` records source URLs, Git blob IDs and SHA256.
The READ ME sheet defines -99 as no cell of that type found in the column. It
does not authorize creation of a synthetic neuron at that position.

`tools/build_optic_column_index.py` verifies source and graph hashes, parses
ME_<side>_col_<hex1>_<hex2>, and checks every positive ID against the full graph.
The workbook contains 892 right and 880 left columns: 1,772 total. Assigned
cells comprise 1,764 L1, 1,299 R7 and 1,329 R8, with no duplicate cell assignments.
All 924 missing cell slots are preserved; eight columns have none of these cells.
The resulting CSVs under `data/derived/malecns_optic_columns` preserve columns,
assignments, missing entries and conflicts; hashes are in
`reports/optic_column_index.json`.

One L1 conflict remains: body 534860 is column ME_L_col_04_16 in the workbook,
but assignedOlHex1/2 is 5/16 in the primary annotation. Neither source is silently
overwritten. This is a source-reconciliation issue, not evidence of a new neuron.

This anatomical index is not an optical ray map or implemented vision. Remaining
work includes R1-R6 receptive fields and neural superposition, geometric mapping
between column coordinates and the body eye's viewing directions, spectral and
temporal phototransduction, and a native renderer/encoder. R7/R8 color labels are
not mapped to arbitrary RGB channels. Numerical telemetry must not enter this
path. No visual input is enabled by this indexing step.

`tools/audit_visual_column_boundary.py` reconciles the author workbook with
the complete 6,098-cell `ol_sensory` boundary. All **1,299 R7** and **1,329
R8** column assignments match sensory body IDs, graph indices and annotated
types. The **1,764 L1** assignments are outside the sensory subset: L1 is a
downstream column neuron. The sensory population partitions exactly into
3,377 R1-R6 without these column assignments, 2,628 column-assigned R7/R8,
86 R7/R8-like cells without a column (85 `R7R8_unclear`, one `R7_unclear`),
and seven HBeyelet cells. The 86 unassigned records and graph identities are
retained in `reports/visual_column_boundary_reconciliation.json`. **Zero
optical rays** are assigned to these neurons. L1 must not be driven as a
receptor input, and the other-specimen eyemap cannot be joined by row order.

## Measured optical reference

`tools/fetch_eyemap_reference.py` acquires 35 author files (1.84 MB) from
reiserlab/eyemap_T4 at 99d2a43123db636cedb55af9ff31a59657e7d17e,
including the GPL license, analysis code and microCT geometry. Source hashes
and Git blob IDs are in `reports/eyemap_reference_sources.json`.
This accompanies Zhao et al., *Eye structure shapes neuron function in
Drosophila motion vision*, DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09276-5.

`tools/export_optical_reference.py` reads the author's 20240701.RData using
rdata 1.0.0 (isolated under build/eye-python). It exports 1,709 smoothed optical
axes: 857 left and 852 right. The author's axes are +X forward, +Y left,
+Z up. Positions retain author units; they are not yet MuJoCo coordinates.
Every row preserves cone index and the corresponding 1-based lens index.
The assignment is a complete permutation without cross-eye matches. Independently
normalizing matched lens-minus-cone positions reproduces stored unsmoothed axes
to 2.22e-16 maximum component error; smoothed axes have unit norms to 2.22e-16.
These checks verify extraction and indexing, not a rerun of the author's smoothing.

Output: `data/derived/optical_reference/rays.csv`, hash-bound in
`reports/optical_reference_export.json`. All rows are disabled and lack MaleCNS
body IDs. This is another specimen; neither 1,772 MaleCNS columns nor 1,709
microCT rays can be silently matched by ordering or resampling. Registration,
acceptance angles, spectral sensitivity and phototransduction remain necessary.

`tools/audit_eye_registration.py` additionally follows the author's
proc_eyemap.R indexing through global lens order, right-eye local lens order,
cone order and the FAFB Mi1 row table. All 778 matched rays agree exactly with
the microCT source. There are 74 unmatched right lenses and one unmatched Mi1
row out of 779. Author Mi1 row numbers are not CATMAID skeleton IDs or MaleCNS
body IDs. The 39 extra rows in ucl_rot_aux are extrapolated boundary points
generated by proc_eyemap.R, not additional measured ommatidia; they are excluded
from the registration export and explicitly counted in the audit. Excluding
synthetic interpolation support does not remove any neuron from our CNS graph.
Evidence: `reports/eye_registration_audit.json` and
`data/derived/optical_reference/fafb_registration.csv`. No MaleCNS registration
is enabled. Establishing landmarks between the two anatomical datasets remains
necessary before borrowing this optical map.

## Same-connectome visual-pathways source to evaluate

Hoeller et al., *The organization of visual pathways in the Drosophila brain*,
Cell (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.014, analyzes visual paths in the male
CNS connectome. The [author repository](https://github.com/reiserlab/visualpathways)
points to predicted anatomical receptive fields and a Neuroglancer
`ME(R)-columns-r-theta` layer for right medulla column retinotopy. The
[MaleCNS project page](https://male-cns.janelia.org/) also links an eyemap
explorer. These are source leads for a same-connectome visual-column check;
they have not been acquired or reconciled with our pinned graph in this
worktree. Predicted downstream receptive fields are not measured ommatidial
optical axes, photoreceptor dynamics, or a direct MaleCNS cell-to-renderer-ray
map. Keep visual-to-CNS assignments disabled until the per-column angular
data, coordinate convention, version and coverage are pinned and checked
against the existing 1,772-column index.

## Same-connectome L1 workbook reconciliation

Two curated L1 column workbooks from `reiserlab/visualpathways` are now pinned
at commit `23f6ac131529b5f56894c6eeb9b88b17894fc00d` under
`data/reference/visualpathways`; their byte hashes and source URLs are in
`source_manifest.json`. `tools/reconcile_visualpathways_l1.py` compares them
with the earlier pinned MaleCNS column index and checks every new L1 ID against
the unchanged full graph. See `reports/visualpathways_l1_column_reconciliation.json`.

The right workbook repeats 28 coordinate/ID rows but its 892 distinct L1
ID-coordinate assignments match the earlier index exactly. The left workbook
has 878 distinct L1 IDs: 869 shared with the earlier 872 IDs, nine new IDs
and three earlier IDs absent. Among the shared IDs, 517 retain the same hex
coordinates and 352 move. At the same coordinate, 345 of 862 comparable
single-valued left entries have different IDs. Most shared-ID shifts are by
one hex coordinate: 146 at (0,+1), 108 at (+1,0), and 79 at (+1,+1).
All new workbook L1 IDs exist in the full graph.

These are source-version differences in a downstream columnar neuron, not
measured optical directions. Neither table replaces the other silently.
In particular, the conflicting left assignments are excluded from any future
accepted neuron-to-ray registration until their provenance and coordinate
convention are resolved. This audit does not enable visual input.

The same audit now checks both maps against `assignedOlHex1/2` in the pinned
primary MaleCNS node annotations, with source hashes verified before use.
Right-side agreement is 892/892 for each map. On the left, the earlier map
agrees for 868/869 cells with both annotation coordinates, whereas the new
map agrees for 520/874 and conflicts for 354/874; four new-map cells and
three earlier-map cells lack both annotation coordinates. The one earlier-map
conflict, body 534860, is resolved by the new workbook at (5,16). These
annotation fields are from the same connectome ecosystem, so agreement is
source consistency rather than independent optical validation. The earlier
left map is better supported overall by this field, but neither source is
silently declared correct for every cell.

## Published retinotopy layer boundary

The Fig. 6c [Neuroglancer state](https://neuroglancer-demo.appspot.com/#!gs://flyem-male-cns/auxiliary-data/retinotopy-tbars/retinotopy-tbars.json)
linked by `reiserlab/visualpathways`, the right-column segment properties,
and the T-bar annotation schema are saved with pinned GCS generations under
`data/reference/visualpathways/retinotopy`. The exact URLs, generations and
SHA-256 values are in its `source_manifest.json`.
`tools/audit_visualpathways_retinotopy_layer.py` checks those files against
the prior column index; see
`reports/visualpathways_retinotopy_layer_boundary.json`.

All 892 right medulla segment labels are exactly the same hex coordinates as
the pinned MaleCNS right-column index. The column layer contains display
colors but no numeric `r` or `theta` property per column. A separate
presynaptic T-bar annotation schema has float32 `hex1`, `hex2`, `r`,
and `theta` fields. The retinotopic T-bar predictions cannot be read as
measured receptor optical axes. A registered, source-backed column-to-ray
calibration and receptor assignment are still required; no visual input is
enabled by this source check.

## Right medulla center-pin geometry

The [visual-system connectome author repository](https://github.com/reiserlab/male-drosophila-visual-system-connectome-code)
provides `results/eyemap/ME_pindata.csv` and the method used to construct
center pins. The data and source notes are pinned by commit and SHA-256 under
`data/reference/malecns_eye_pins`. The construction includes smoothing
and filling missing columns; the centerline is a processed anatomical object.

`tools/audit_right_medulla_pins.py` checks all 107,932 source rows:
the same 892 right medulla hex columns each have exactly 121 distinct
depth bins (0–120), finite coordinates and positive step lengths. It exports
the endpoints and normalized **internal medulla** depth directions to
`data/derived/right_medulla_pins_v1/axes.csv`. The minimum and median
endpoint-chord/centerline-arc ratios are 0.9208 and 0.9938. Source and
derived hashes are in `reports/malecns_right_medulla_pin_geometry.json`.

These centerlines provide a same-connectome anatomical geometry constraint
for future eye-to-brain registration. Their endpoint directions are not
measured viewing directions. The author coordinate note says the hex origin
is arbitrary outside the eye; it does not provide a rigid transform from
the source XYZ coordinates to the FlyMimic head or ommatidial optical axes.
No MaleCNS visual neuron is stimulated from these pins.

## Direct R1–R6 to L1 anatomical paths

`tools/audit_r16_l1_column_edges.py` verifies the accepted complete graph
and source column index hashes, then preserves every direct graph row from
the 3,377 `R1-R6` cells to the 1,764 author-assigned L1 cells. The
3,267 rows contain 102,940 annotated contacts and no cross-side row.
For 3,217 receptors there is exactly one assigned L1 target column;
138 have none and 22 have multiple (20 with two, one with three, one with
seven). Only 803 of the 1,764 assigned L1 cells receive at least one such
direct R1–R6 row in this accepted graph; 961 receive none.
See `reports/malecns_r16_l1_column_edges.json` and source-indexed rows
under `data/derived/r16_l1_column_edges_v1`.

A single target is an unambiguous direct anatomical path to an L1 medulla
hex column **within this graph**, not proof of the receptor's ommatidial
position or viewing direction. The graph's `minconf=0.5` threshold and
published reconstruction coverage limit negative claims from absent rows.
No highest-weight fallback is used for multiple targets, and all 3,377
receptors stay in the full CNS. No photoreceptor-to-ray stimulation is enabled.

## Two laminar paths from R1–R6

`tools/audit_r16_l1_l2_paths.py` adds direct R1–R6→L2 rows from the
unchanged full graph, using primary `assignedOlHex1/2` and the source
`L2_L/R` instance side. It checks all graph and prior L1 hashes.
The 1,767 L2 cells with both hex fields receive 3,274 direct rows
(107,452 contacts) from R1–R6 across 811 L2 cells.

At the receptor level, 3,132 of 3,377 have a single L1 column and a
single L2 column that **agree exactly**. Another 69 have only a single
L1 path, 76 only a single L2 path, 61 neither, and 39 have multiple
or mixed paths. There are zero conflicts where both paths are single.
Three L2-only paths point to two left hex coordinates absent from the
earlier accepted column set; they remain marked as outside that index.
Every direct L2 edge, source row and receptor status is retained under
`data/derived/r16_l1_l2_paths_v1`; hashes and counts are in
`reports/malecns_r16_l1_l2_paths.json`.

This is strong **internal anatomical consistency** for the 3,132
two-path cells, not an independent optical measurement. L1 and L2
coordinates and the synapses come from the same connectome ecosystem.
The receiver column can support future input routing only after a
source-backed column-to-optical-ray and receptor-stimulus calibration.
No missing, multiply connected or outside-index receptor is discarded.

## Other-specimen FAFB hex-to-ray reference

`tools/export_fafb_hex_rays.py` joins the author's complete 852-lens right-eye
`lens_ixy` hex table to the already verified 778 FAFB Mi1/lens/microCT
matches. It exports `data/derived/optical_reference/fafb_hex_rays.csv`,
with the measured ray vector and both FAFB grid coordinates for each matched
lens. Source and output SHA-256 values are in
`reports/fafb_hex_ray_export.json`. The 74 unmatched right lenses remain
unfilled. The author's FAFB lens grid spans x=-17..20, y=-17..18.

This is a useful measured optical reference for registration research, but
its hex indices are from a different specimen and coordinate origin from the
MaleCNS right medulla's `hex1/hex2`. The export has zero MaleCNS body IDs.
A source-backed anatomical landmark transform, side/orientation check and
held-out registration error are required before pairing any of these 778
rays with the MaleCNS receptors or columns.

## Shape-only registration ambiguity

`tools/audit_fafb_malecns_hex_registration_ambiguity.py` compares the
complete 852-lens FAFB hex grid with the 892 MaleCNS right medulla
columns under a **hypothesized** 12-way axial hex symmetry family and
every integer translation. The result is a diagnostic of what grid
contours alone can identify; it is not a biological registration.
Source and output hashes are in
`reports/fafb_malecns_hex_registration_ambiguity.json`.

The best two candidates use the same reflection with shifts (18,18)
and (19,19), covering 846 and 844 FAFB lenses respectively. Both put
all 778 measured FAFB rays on MaleCNS hex coordinates, but on the 743
MaleCNS coordinates shared by those ray sets, the assigned ray vectors
differ by a median 4.87° (90th percentile 6.10°). Other orientation
hypotheses also have large overlap. A high contour overlap cannot select
homologous ommatidia or determine the transform. The paper's equator and
central-meridian landmarks and an independent held-out registration
check are needed before accepting any ray. No candidate is enabled or
written into the MaleCNS sensory map.

## Equator-landmark coverage check

The optical-map paper identified the equatorial medulla region through
lamina cartridges with seven or eight outer photoreceptors instead of
the usual six, using reconstruction and morphology in addition to
connectivity. `tools/audit_r16_l1_equator_boundary.py` counts distinct
direct R1–R6 inputs for **every** one of the 1,764 author-assigned L1
cells in the accepted `minconf=0.5` MaleCNS graph. Per-cell counts and
source hashes are in `data/derived/r16_l1_equator_boundary_v1/per_l1_counts.csv`
and `reports/malecns_r16_l1_equator_boundary.json`.

The graph has 961 L1 cells with zero direct R1–R6 rows, 242 with six,
24 with seven or eight, and ten with more than eight. These rows do not
provide a near-complete cartridge census across the eye and therefore
do not establish an equator line. The 7/8-count cells are retained for
future source review, not promoted to landmarks. A finer source table
or original reconstructed cartridge morphology is required.
