# Photoreceptor high-flux numerical diagnosis

The pinned VisTrans photoreceptor equations are tested here without changing their biological parameters. This is a numerical sensitivity study, not a biological calibration or permission to use a high-flux response for training.

The author membrane kernel advances each gate with forward Euler at 0.01 ms inside a 0.1 ms photoreceptor step. For the inactivation gate `si`, its published time constant is `113 * exp(-(-71-V)^2 / 841)` ms. At the highest voltage reached by the bounded diagnostic, about +37.28 mV, that time constant is roughly 0.000100 ms. The original Euler step is about 100 times larger, so its gate can leave [0,1]. Tenfold smaller Euler substeps (0.001 ms) still failed at 542 ms in the strongest flash, versus 538 ms for the original. Three lower doses completed. The exact source, binary, traces and comparison report were verified in the private HF archive at revision `0da1963e46b490a518750f3bbffba739084895bd`, manifest `manifests/14245b2a7f6c8a2dd901116d720cd603b5511f4450bb8dcd3f995938b8b124f3.json`.

`tools/build_photon_exact_gates.py` builds a separate HH diagnostic that solves the scalar gate ODE exactly over each existing 0.01 ms substep at fixed voltage: `x_new = x_inf + (x_old - x_inf) * exp(-dt/tau_x)`. This preserves the author's gate equations and voltage Euler update; it changes the numerical integrator. For an initial gate and equilibrium in [0,1] and positive time constant, the update stays in [0,1]. It is not silently substituted for the baseline library.

With 64 receptors, 30,000 microvilli per receptor, seed 19503, 500 ms darkness, 10 ms flash, and 500 ms darkness, the exact-gate diagnostic completed all 1,010 ms at four rates: 10,000, 100,000, 1,000,000, and 10,000,000 photons/s. The corresponding nominal 10 ms doses were 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 photons. The strongest dose had a 92.10 mV rise from preflash and reached a maximum individual-cell voltage of +37.28 mV. Relative to the original baseline, root-mean-square voltage differences on the three completed low-dose episodes were 0.0096, 0.0415, and 0.0556 mV. The high-dose baseline failed early, so its common prefix is not a converged reference. Source, traces, build and report are SHA-verified in private HF at revision `38049691cc1f74677a37731315ab74eddd63281b`, manifest `manifests/ad859b63037b7dde12056fca69aa2cc609cec316704f5ad1115741ef2223f17c.json`.

At 6091 receptors, 30,000 microvilli each, 50,000 photons/s, and 100 steps, paired isolated timing was 6.58426 s for baseline and 6.58473 s for exact gates. The maximum observed-state difference was 1.34×10⁻⁶. Thus this correction has negligible cost in the measured low-flux full-retina case; it does not solve the 657× real-time throughput bottleneck. The speed report is archived at private HF revision `2174c1ecf2b7ea647fcafba0d9ee6324964964e5`, manifest `manifests/ef90db3ccaec73bfe1555aace0b0efd1861a7d03d01a491a186b54e4191ddc9f.json`.

The model's 92 mV rise at the nominal strong dose exceeds the typical 40–60 mV in-vivo response cited in `VISUAL_CAUSALITY.md`, but the model's input photon count has not been calibrated to photons absorbed by that in-vivo receptor, and temperature/specimen/readout differ. The numerical failure is removed in this diagnostic; biological agreement or disagreement under matched conditions remains untested.

A further convergence probe kept exact gate updates but reduced the voltage Euler substep from 0.01 to 0.001 ms. For the 100-photon nominal flash, the mean peak changed by −0.00038 mV and the trace RMSE was 0.0081 mV. For the 100,000-photon nominal flash, the mean peak changed from 92.10 to 93.72 mV (1.62 mV) and the paired trace RMSE was 2.61 mV; the maximum individual voltage difference was 44.20 mV. The two stochastic trajectories may diverge after small state differences, so the large pointwise maximum alone does not prove solver nonconvergence. It does show that the current single-seed high-dose result is not yet a reliable quantitative biological target. The source, fine-step binary, paired traces and report are SHA-verified at private HF revision `e95fbf3fa964cd9de904ce0814ae9d3dbfba4246`, manifest `manifests/0112bd521505d82bbe28385dbf98668dc18d7f43425be0ee03a79742381d43f2.json`.

Before adopting the numerical variant, test high-flux distributions over independent seeds and further voltage-step refinement, calibrate stimulus units, compare waveform and noise to independent physiological recordings, and rerun visual CNS checks. `biological_gate_passed` remains false.

## Electrical current coupling discovered after the gate test

The original `sum_current` kernel computes the light-induced current from the membrane voltage **once per 0.1 ms receptor tick**. The HH kernel then holds that current constant over its 10 membrane substeps. This zero-order hold can keep driving a strongly illuminated receptor upward after its voltage crosses the TRP reversal potential of 0 mV. Finer HH steps alone do not restore the missing voltage-current feedback. Across three additional seeds at 10^7 photons/s, both exact-gate integrations with held current exceeded 0 mV in all 64 test receptors; the maximum individual voltage was +38 to +40 mV, and the population peak rise varied about 92 to 96 mV. The seeds, source and complete traces were SHA-verified at private HF revision `1342d57b506c87e6eb8f48c800057d17f402163e`, manifest `manifests/0be8f1e81c4404b898e1ad4d4ca1f7fa35c581997eb8129fe12bfc1c032dd0fd.json`.

`tools/build_photon_coupled_current.py` builds another isolated diagnostic. It retains the number of open channels over each 0.1 ms tick, as in the original, but passes their conductance to HH and evaluates `I_TRP = G_open * max(-V, 0)` at **each 0.01 ms membrane substep**. The exact-gate integrator remains in use. This changes the coupling schedule, not the phototransduction reactions or membrane ODE parameters. With one seed, the 100-photon nominal flash peak matched the held-current version within 0.001 mV, with 0.021 mV trace RMSE. At the 100,000-photon nominal flash, the population peak rise decreased from 92.10 to 81.19 mV; the maximum individual voltage fell from +37.28 to −0.80 mV. The diagnostic build, raw traces, comparison and full-retina timing were SHA-verified at private HF revision `4504dad5f8ecfe59ba88aacc784c9b01e3a28ac7`, manifest `manifests/87184b393e1d8a4db8b4f0b7cda9aa28708f4ee59c71b6c3186129ed5990b2e6.json`.

Three fresh high-flux seeds repeated this comparison. With the coupled current, no test receptor crossed 0 mV and each population peak rise was 81.185–81.187 mV; with held current all 64 crossed 0 mV on each seed. These observations cover this high-flux protocol, not an all-input mathematical guarantee. The paired traces and report are SHA-verified at private HF revision `51c00d613bab95aa6697520bd8af97bb25dde184`, manifest `manifests/712cfd11eb523747b21f9b8e792757c38126bf8a9275578fc49c0256d2669eeb.json`. Full-retina 100-step timing at 50,000 photons/s changed from 6.5827 to 6.6029 s, a 0.3% slowdown in this pair. It remains unsuitable for mass training at full microvillus resolution.

This is a causal numerical correction candidate, **not** a validated biological model. The strong response still rises roughly 81 mV, and the mapping from the paper's estimated photons to this model's input is unresolved. A subsequent matched full-CNS test kept the configured visual bridge fixed and changed only the receptor library: L1/L2 light and dark population-mean responses differed by at most 0.008 mV across the flash protocol. The paired report is SHA-verified at private HF revision `0ebb7ec54ac632d46bf7bf85f95986f3b21e04f5`, manifest `manifests/a451fa65e2aab77d8c79861987340af4ed10b017b65dca96dba9a2c6de598bde.json`; see `VISUAL_CAUSALITY.md` for the controlled comparison and correction of an earlier cross-bridge comparison. The fast sensory surrogate was fitted to the previous held-current teacher, so its reported fidelity cannot be carried over to this candidate. `biological_gate_passed` remains false.
